Ask five vendors what school management software costs in Pakistan and you will get five answers that cannot be compared, because they are not quoting the same thing. One prices per student, one prices per month, one is free until you hit a limit, and one will not tell you at all until you sit through a demo. Here is how to read those numbers.

The two pricing models, and why they differ so much

Almost every vendor in Pakistan uses one of two structures.

Per student, per month. Across the market this runs roughly Rs 15 to Rs 50 per student per month, with some premium products quoting up to Rs 100. It scales cleanly, which suits a growing school, but it punishes you for enrolling children — the thing you are otherwise trying to do.

A fixed monthly plan, banded by size. Published entry plans start from about Rs 1,500 to Rs 3,000 a month for small schools, rising to the Rs 10,000–20,000 range for a few hundred students. This is easier to budget against and is what most principals actually prefer, because it is a line item that does not move.

A handful of vendors also offer a genuinely free tier — typically capped at 50 or 100 students. If you are a small academy, that is worth taking seriously.

Convert everything to one number before you compare

Take each quote and work out cost per student per month. A Rs 10,000 fixed plan for 400 children is Rs 25 per child. A Rs 40 per-student quote for the same school is Rs 16,000. Same software category, 60% difference — invisible until you do the division.

What the first quote leaves out

The subscription is rarely the whole bill. Before you compare anything, ask about all six of these in writing:

  • Setup and data entry. Importing your student list, and enrolling every fingerprint if you are using biometrics. Some vendors include it; some bill it as a one-time fee of several thousand rupees.
  • Training. At minimum one session for the office staff. Ask whether follow-up sessions cost extra when you hire someone new.
  • SMS and WhatsApp charges. These are never included in the subscription — see our breakdown of WhatsApp versus SMS costs. Watch for vendors reselling you SMS credits at a markup rather than letting you pay the gateway directly.
  • Hardware. A ZKTeco fingerprint or RFID terminal is typically Rs 15,000–45,000, one time, per gate.
  • Per-campus fees. If you run branches, check whether each one is charged separately.
  • Annual renewal or "maintenance". Some contracts carry this on top of the monthly cost.

Why the cheapest quote often costs more

There is a floor below which a vendor cannot support you properly, and in this market it is lower than people expect. A school paying Rs 1,500 a month is contributing perhaps Rs 18,000 a year. That does not fund a support person answering the phone at 7:45am when the reader will not start on a Monday.

The three places the difference shows up are always the same: whether anyone picks up when something breaks, whether your data is actually backed up on a schedule someone checks, and whether the person who installed it is still trading in two years. A school that saves Rs 5,000 a month and loses four hours a week to workarounds has not saved anything.

Equally, the most expensive quote is not automatically the best. Ask what the extra money buys in concrete terms — named support contact, response time, included training — and if the answer is vague, it is not buying anything.

Getting comparable quotes

Send every vendor the same five facts and you will get answers you can actually line up side by side:

  1. Your exact student count today, and your realistic count in two years.
  2. How many campuses and how many entrance gates.
  3. Whether you want biometric, card, or on-screen marking — or a mix by section.
  4. Roughly how many parent messages a month you expect to send.
  5. Whether you want it hosted by them or on your own server.

Then ask for one number: total cost for the first twelve months, everything included. Any vendor who will not put that in an email is telling you something.

Is it worth it at all?

The honest arithmetic: a mid-range plan for a 300-student school is somewhere around Rs 7,500–15,000 a month. Against that, weigh the teaching time recovered from roll call, the office hours not spent counting registers at month end, and the fee-paying parents who stay because they get told the same morning their child did not arrive.

For most private schools the parent-communication piece alone justifies it. Attendance is the one thing every parent cares about daily, and it is the cheapest trust you will ever buy.

What the monthly fee is actually buying

If a vendor hosts the software for you, the subscription covers four things that are easy to overlook because none of them are features:

  • The server, and someone keeping it running. Attendance software that is down at 8am is worse than useless.
  • Backups taken on a schedule and, crucially, checked. Ask how often, where they are kept, and when the vendor last restored one to prove it works.
  • Updates, including the security patches you will never hear about.
  • Support — a person, in your timezone, who answers.

A one-time licence for software you host yourself removes the monthly fee, but it moves all four of those onto you. That is a real trade, not a saving. It makes sense if you already employ someone technical, and very little sense if you do not.

A worked comparison

Three quotes for the same 300-student school, converted to one number:

Rs 40 per studentRs 12,000Rs 40Rs 144,000
Rs 8,000 fixed + Rs 20,000 setupRs 8,000Rs 27Rs 116,000
Rs 1,500 fixed, support billed hourlyRs 1,500Rs 5Rs 18,000 + support

The third looks unbeatable until you ask how many support hours a first year realistically takes. At a typical hourly rate, twenty hours closes most of the gap, and you have carried the risk yourself the whole time.

The second is usually the honest middle. Note that the setup fee makes year one look worse than year two — always compare a full twelve months, not a monthly figure.

Before you sign

  1. Is there a lock-in? Monthly rolling is normal in this market. Be wary of a twelve-month commitment taken upfront.
  2. Can you export everything? Get it in writing that you can take the full database out, in a standard format, whenever you want.
  3. What happens at the band edge? If you are on an up-to-400 plan and enrol your 401st student mid-term, what exactly happens?
  4. Is there a trial? A month with your own students tells you more than any demo. Several vendors now offer this; take it.

Common questions

How much does school management software cost in Pakistan?

Most vendors charge either Rs 15 to Rs 50 per student per month, or a fixed monthly plan banded by school size. Published entry plans start from around Rs 1,500 to Rs 3,000 a month for small schools, rising into the Rs 10,000 to Rs 20,000 range for a few hundred students. A few vendors offer a free tier capped at 50 or 100 students.

What is a fair price per student per month?

Rs 15 to Rs 50 covers most of the market. Below about Rs 15 per student, question whether the vendor can fund real support. Above Rs 50, ask specifically what the extra buys: named support contact, guaranteed response time, included training.

Are SMS and WhatsApp charges included in the subscription?

Almost never. Messaging is billed by the gateway or by Meta, separately from the software. You should pay the provider directly rather than buying credits from your software vendor at a markup.

Is there free school management software in Pakistan?

Yes, several vendors offer a free tier, usually capped at 50 or 100 students. For a small academy that can be genuinely sufficient. Check what happens at the cap, and whether your data can be exported if you outgrow it.
