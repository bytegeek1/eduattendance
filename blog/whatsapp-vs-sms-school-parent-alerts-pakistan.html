WhatsApp vs SMS for School Parent Alerts

Every attendance system promises to "alert parents". The interesting question is on which channel, because WhatsApp and SMS behave completely differently, cost completely differently, and fail completely differently. Most Pakistani schools should be using both, for different things.

Which one actually reaches the parent?

WhatsApp is close to universal among smartphone-owning parents in Pakistani cities, and messages get read. It renders Urdu script properly, supports longer text, and shows you delivery and read receipts, so you know whether a message about an absence actually landed.

SMS reaches every handset ever made. No smartphone, no data connection, no app — it simply arrives. In smaller towns, and among grandparents and drivers who are often the daytime contact, this still matters more than people in Lahore or Karachi assume.

Government programmes have gone both ways. Punjab rolled out daily SMS alerts on attendance to parents of government school students, on the reasonable basis that SMS is the only channel guaranteed to reach everyone. Sindh's school monitoring app, funded by the EU with UNICEF's support, also alerts parents by SMS when a child skips school.

The Urdu point catches schools out. A standard SMS holds 160 Latin characters, but only 70 when it contains Urdu script, because it switches to a different encoding. An Urdu absence notice that looks short can bill as three messages. WhatsApp has no such penalty.

Use the official WhatsApp API, not a browser session

This is the single most important thing in this article.

There are two ways a vendor can send WhatsApp messages for you. The right way is the official WhatsApp Cloud API from Meta: your school gets a verified business number, messages go through approved templates, and everything is above board.

The wrong way is a server quietly logged into WhatsApp Web, automating a normal account. It is cheaper to build, which is why it exists. It also breaks every time WhatsApp changes something, and it risks the number being banned outright — taking your entire parent communication channel with it, usually on a morning when you need it.

Ask the vendor one question: "Is this the official WhatsApp Cloud API, and will the number be verified in our school's name?" If the answer is anything other than a clear yes, assume it is a browser session.

The sensible setup: both, for different jobs

Rather than choosing, split by urgency and by parent:

  • WhatsApp as the default for arrival confirmations, absence notices, monthly attendance summaries and general notices. Richer, cheaper at volume, and read.
  • SMS as the fallback for any guardian with no WhatsApp on their number, and as the second channel for genuinely urgent messages where you cannot risk a parent having no data connection.

Good software lets you set this per school, and ideally per guardian. It should also let you send on both channels for the same event without the parent receiving two identical messages by accident.

The guarantee nobody asks about

If the job that sends absence notices runs hourly, what stops a parent getting the same message at 9am, 10am and 11am?

The answer should be that the system records which guardian has been told about which child on which date on which channel, and refuses to send twice. That has to be enforced in the database, not by assuming the job only ever runs once. Ask for it explicitly — a system that spams parents on a bad day will lose you more goodwill than not messaging at all.

The same logic applies to approved leave. If a parent has applied for leave and the school approved it, the absence alert should not fire at all. Check that the two features actually know about each other.

What a good attendance message says

The channel matters less than the wording, and most schools get this wrong by being either too curt or too formal. A parent reading on a phone at 8:20am needs three facts and nothing else: which child, what happened, and when.

Ayesha Khan was marked absent today, 30 August, at morning roll call. If this is unexpected, please contact the school office on 0313 3398883.

That works because it names the child (parents often have more than one at the school), dates it, says which register it came from, and gives an action. Compare it with "Your ward is absent" — which tells a parent of three children almost nothing.

Three things worth building into your templates:

  • Always name the child. Never "your child" or "your ward".
  • Say when the mark was taken, not when the message was sent. A parent needs to know whether this is the 8am register or period four.
  • Give a number to call, and make sure someone answers it. An alert that prompts a question with nowhere to ask it creates work rather than saving it.

Getting a WhatsApp Business number verified

Schools are often put off by this, but it is a form-filling exercise rather than a technical one. You will need a number that is not currently on a personal WhatsApp account, your school's registration documents, and a Meta Business account. Your software vendor should do the connection; you supply the documents.

Two practical notes. Use a number the school controls, not a staff member's personal phone — people leave. And expect the display name to be reviewed, so use the school's real registered name rather than a marketing slogan.

Parents you cannot reach

Every school has some. In practice the gaps are: numbers that were wrong on the admission form, numbers that have changed, and children whose daytime guardian is a grandparent or driver with a basic handset.

Three things that help more than any software setting. Verify guardian numbers at the start of every term, not once at admission. Allow more than one guardian number per child, so a father's and mother's numbers both work. And keep a report of which parents have never received a message successfully — that list is short, actionable, and nobody ever looks at it.

Common questions

Is WhatsApp or SMS better for school attendance alerts?

WhatsApp is better for most messages: it renders Urdu correctly, costs less at volume, and gives you read receipts. SMS is better as a fallback because it reaches every handset with no smartphone or data connection needed. Most Pakistani schools should use WhatsApp as the default and SMS as the backup.

How much do WhatsApp messages to parents cost?

Meta bills the official WhatsApp Cloud API per 24-hour conversation rather than per message, so several messages to the same parent in one day cost once. Rates vary by country and template category. You pay Meta directly, not your software vendor.

Why do Urdu SMS messages cost more?

A standard SMS fits 160 Latin characters, but Urdu script forces a different encoding that allows only 70 characters per message. A short-looking Urdu notice can therefore bill as two or three messages. WhatsApp has no such limit.

Is it safe to use a vendor that sends WhatsApp through WhatsApp Web?

No. Automating a normal WhatsApp account through a browser session breaks whenever WhatsApp changes, and risks the number being banned, which would take your whole parent communication channel with it. Insist on the official WhatsApp Cloud API with a number verified in your school name.
